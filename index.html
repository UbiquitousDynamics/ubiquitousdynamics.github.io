RESEARCH FOCUS

Which resource should
a machine use next?

For each request: a deterministic algorithm, a small local model, a large local model or human input? We are exploring how to improve task success within energy and latency budgets, while limiting errors and demands on users.

01

Efficient

Allocate computation, energy and connectivity to the simplest effective solution.

02

Natural

Use natural language with local AI. Offline operation is a design requirement.

03

Human oversight

Defer uncertain decisions to a person, with explicit limits on how often the system asks for help.

Working hypothesis. Resource selection could reduce energy per completed task while maintaining comparable success to strong baselines, within fixed latency and user-request limits. Evaluation will include selection overhead and human effort; a request for help alone will not count as a completed task. This remains a research proposal.

WHERE IT STARTED

From solar mobility
to Physical AI.

Could a solar vehicle support natural interaction with fewer electronic components, even offline?

Work with Onda Solare turned this question into experiments in voice interaction and local AI. Ubiquitous Dynamics grew from that work.

HOW WE WORK

Domain. Method.
Criterion.

Three principles guide every project in the laboratory.

Physical AI & Autonomous Systems

The domain. We explore intelligent machines that perceive, interact and act in the physical world: vehicles, robots and the software that guides them.

Learning by Building

The method. Students and junior professionals turn STEM knowledge into practical skills by writing software, integrating hardware and testing real systems in multidisciplinary teams.

Eco-Pragmatism

The criterion. Test usefulness and resource costs in context. Use a simple algorithm when AI is unnecessary, and build systems that support people within ecological limits.
